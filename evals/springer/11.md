# Introduction
Introduction
According to the global cancer data in 2022, gastric cancer (GC) is the fifth most frequently diagnosed cancer and the fifth leading cause of cancer death worldwide [1]. GC poses a significant threat to human health and is a leading cause of cancer-related mortality. Lymphovascular invasion (LVI), characterized by the infiltration of tumor cells into lymphatic and/or blood vessels [2], and perineural invasion (PNI), which involves tumor cell invasion of nerves [3], are both indicators of aggressive tumor behavior and are associated with higher recurrence and metastasis rates in GC [4,5,6]. The presence of pathological LVI/PNI has been identified as an independent predictor of poor prognosis, with lower overall survival and disease-free survival rates in both early and advanced GC patients [2, 4,5,6]. Some studies have even suggested that LVI/PNI status could serve as a stratification factor for personalized adjuvant therapy post-surgery in GC patients [6, 7]. Accurately assessing LVI/PNI status is crucial for predicting GC prognosis and tailoring individualized treatment plans. However, LVI/PNI status in GC patients is typically confirmed through postoperative pathology, highlighting the urgent need for accurate preoperative identification of LVI/PNI status.

Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) are a key component of the tumor microenvironment and are known to produce tumor-promoting growth factors that enhance tumor cell proliferation, angiogenesis, metastasis, and overall progression [8]. Fibroblast activation protein (FAP) is significantly overexpressed in CAFs of gastric cancer compared to its minimal expression in normal gastric tissues [9, 10]. Radiolabeled FAP inhibitors (FAPI), such as [68Ga]Ga-FAPI-04, 18F-FAPI-42, [18F]AlF-NOTA-FAPI-04, and 18F-FAPI-74, have been developed as positron emission tomography (PET) tracers and have shown high uptake in gastric primary lesions and metastatic lesions, particularly peritoneal metastases [11,12,13,14]. To our knowledge, the predictive value of FAPI PET/CT in determining LVI/PNI status in GC patients has not been previously evaluated. This study aims to explore whether 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT can serve as a non-invasive method for preoperative assessment of LVI/PNI status in newly diagnosed GC.

# Materials and Methods
Patient enrollment
This study was a retrospective analysis conducted using data from a previously established prospective database, which was registered under the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2200059004). A total of 72 patients with newly diagnosed GC were enrolled in this investigation. The principal inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) patients diagnosed with GC who had not yet received any tumor treatment; (2) patients who consented to undergo 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT scans and provided written informed consent; (3) all patients subsequently underwent radical surgery for GC following their 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT scans. Based on the postoperative pathological findings, patients exhibiting LVI and/or PNI were categorized into the positive group (LVI/PNI-positive), while those without evidence of LVI or PNI were assigned to the negative group (LVI/PNI-negative). LVI is defined as the presence of tumor cells within the lumen of lymphatic or blood vessels, confirmed by immunohistochemical staining for D2-40 or CD31 [2]. PNI is diagnosed by the identification of tumor cells infiltrating nerve fibers or perineural spaces, as confirmed by histopathological examination [3]. The histological grade was determined based on preoperative gastroscopic biopsy pathology.

18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT imaging protocol
As reported previously, the synthesis and radiolabeling of 18F-FAPI-42 were conducted automatically on the AllInOne module, with the radiochemical purity exceeding 95% [15]. Patients were advised to fast for approximately 2 h to minimize hepatobiliary excretion prior to the administration of 18F-FAPI-42 at a dosage of 0.04–0.06 mCi/kg. Whole-body PET/CT scans were initiated approximately 60 min after the injection of 18F-FAPI-42. 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT scans were performed using a total-body PET/CT scanner (uEXPLORER, United Imaging Healthcare, Shanghai, China). PET imaging was acquired in three-dimensional mode with a 5-minute acquisition single bed position. An unenhanced low-dose CT scan, with settings of 120 kV voltage, 80 mA current, and a slice thickness of 3.0 mm, was utilized for attenuation correction, reconstruction, and anatomical localization of lesions. All data were reconstructed using the OSEM-PSF-TOF algorithm [16].

18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT image analysis
The acquired CT and PET images were sent to the Ukenetics Workstation for registration, fusion and measurement. The 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT images were analyzed by two experienced nuclear medicine physicians. Primary lesions were identified as positive with the abnormal accumulation of 18F-FAPI-42, which were higher than those of the adjacent normal tissue. A volume of interest (VOI) was manually delineated around the primary lesion and adjusted to coincide with the lesion edge at three planes for semiquantitative analysis. SUVmax and SUVmean of the primary gastric lesion were subsequently generated. The tumor-to-background ratio (TBR) was calculated by dividing the SUVmax of primary lesion by the SUVmean of the tumor-free gastric tissue.

Statistical analysis
Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS Statistics version 22 (IBM). Quantitative variables were presented as medians with ranges (from the minimum to maximum), and qualitative variables were presented as raw numbers and proportions. The nonparametric test (Mann-Whitney U test) and the chi-squared test (or Fisher exact test) were used to compare the quantitative variables and qualitative variables in two groups with different LVI/PNI status, respectively. The receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and the area under the curve (AUC) were used to evaluate the value of PET parameters in predicting LVI/PNI status. The two-tailed p value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

A nomogram was created using significant features identified from the multivariate stepwise regression analysis. The calibration curve and Spiegelhalter’s Z-test were utilized to assess the nomogram’s fit to the dataset, ensuring its predictive accuracy. ROC curve and AUC were used to evaluate the discrimination capability of the nomogram. The clinical utility of the nomogram was further appraised through decision curve analysis (DCA), which quantified the net benefits at various threshold probabilities. The treat-none scheme assumed all the GC patients with LVI/PNI-negative and the treat-all patients scheme assumed all GC patients with LVI/PNI-positive.

# Results
Patient characteristics and 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT image results
Seventy-two patients with newly diagnosed GC, comprising 43 males and 29 females with a median age of 60 years (ranging from 29 to 70 years), who underwent 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT, were included in this study. Within this cohort, 27 patients (27/72, 37.5%) were classified as LVI/PNI-negative, whereas 45 patients (45/72, 62.5%) were identified as LVI/PNI-positive. Among the LVI/PNI-positive group, 24 patients exhibited both LVI and PNI, 18 patients had only PNI, and 3 patients had only LVI.

In 72 patients with GC, 11 patients (11/72, 15.3%) showed negative results on 18F-FAPI-42 PET scans, and all these patients were LVI/PNI-negative. Conversely, 61 patients (61/72, 84.7%) demonstrated positive 18F-FAPI-42 PET findings, which included 16 LVI/PNI-negative patients and 45 LVI/PNI-positive patients. In the 72 patients with primary gastric lesions, the SUVmax, SUVmean, and TBR values were 6.71 (range: 0.48–18.29), 3.28 (range: 0.35–6.80), and 13.27 (range: 1.09–44.23), respectively. Among the 61 patients who exhibited positive findings on 18F-FAPI-42 PET imaging, the SUVmax, SUVmean, and TBR were 8.15 (range: 0.78–18.29), 3.68 (range: 0.56–6.80), and 17.63 (range: 3.17–44.23), respectively.

Comparison of clinicopathological factors and 18F-FAPI-42 PET parameters in GC patients with different LVI/PNI status
The comparison of clinicopathological factors and PET parameters in two groups of patients with or without LVI/PNI was detailed in Table 1. No significant differences were observed between LVI/PNI-positive group and LVI/PNI-negative groups in terms of the clinical pathological factors, including age, sex, serum tumor markers, tumor location and histological grade (all p > 0.05). Nevertheless, clinical T stage and clinical N stage were statistically significantly between LVI/PNI-positive group and LVI/PNI-negative groups (p = 0.006 and p = 0.001, respectively). The three 18F-FAPI-42 PET parameters were all significantly higher in patients with LVI/PNI-positive status than those in patients with LVI/PNI-negative status, including SUVmax [9.15 (1.33–18.29) vs. 2.18 (0.48–8.60), p < 0.001)], SUVmean [4.36 (0.98–6.80) vs. 1.30 (0.35–4.35), p < 0.001)] and TBR [21.31 (4.29–44.23) vs. 4.11 (1.09–19.55), p < 0.001)]. Furthermore, in the subgroup patients with positive PET findings, the three 18F-FAPI-42 PET parameters were also significantly higher among patients with LVI/PNI-positive status compared to those with LVI/PNI-negative status (Table 2, with all p-values < 0.001).

Predictive value of 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT parameters for LVI/PNI status
ROC analysis was performed to assess the predictive accuracy of 18F-FAPI-42 PET parameters in determining the LVI/PNI status in patients with GC. The discriminative power of each 18F-FAPI-42 PET parameter was presented in Table 3; Fig. 1. These parameters demonstrated significant evaluative value, with AUC values of 0.932 (95% CI, 0.877–0.987) for SUVmax, 0.923 (95% CI, 0.861–0.984) for SUVmean, and 0.925 (95% CI, 0.865–0.985) for TBR. The predictive values of these three 18F-FAPI-42 PET parameters showed a similarly strong discriminating capability in predicting the LVI/PNI status, with SUVmax exhibiting a slightly higher AUC value.

Furthermore, the optimal cutoff values derived from the ROC analysis for the 18F-FAPI-42 PET parameters were as follows, SUVmax at 3.86 with sensitivity and specificity of 93.3% and 81.5%, respectively; SUVmean at 2.04 with sensitivity and specificity of 93.3% and 81.5%, respectively; and TBR at 9.75 with sensitivity and specificity of 91.1% and 81.5%, respectively. A GC patient with LVI/PNI negative status was presented in Fig. 2, and the three PET parameters were all below the optimal cutoff values. A GC patient with LVI/PNI positive status was displayed in Fig. 3, and the PET parameters were all above the optimal cutoff values.

The predictive value of combining the three 18F-FAPI-42 PET parameters for LVI/PNI status was further explored. The resulting AUC value was 0.934 (95%CI, 0.880–0.988), with a sensitivity of 88.90% and a specificity of 85.20%. The predictive value of combined parameters was not significantly different from the predictive value of the individual SUVmax, SUVmean, and TBR parameters.

Multivariable Stepwise regression analysis and nomogram assessment for predicting LVI/PNI status in GC patients
A multivariate stepwise regression analysis was conducted, including all clinicopathological factors and 18F-FAPI-42 PET parameters. A multi-collinearity test indicated the presence of multi-collinearity in PET parameters (Variance Inflation Factor > 10). Given that SUVmax was relatively stable and less susceptible to subjective measurement errors, which also had the highest AUC value among the three 18F-FAPI-42 PET parameters, we retained this parameter and excluded SUVmean and TBR to mitigate multi-collinearity. The subsequent multivariate stepwise regression analysis identified histological grade and SUVmax as independent risk factors for LVI/PNI-positive status in GC patients (both p < 0.001).

For nomogram construction, histological grade and SUVmax were utilized. The nomogram featured a scale at the top to allocate points to each factor, with each patient’s total point score translating into a statistical probability of LVI/PNI presence using the scale at the bottom of the nomogram. The corresponding mark on the “Total points” line indicates the likelihood of LVI/PNI status (Fig. 4). The nomogram’s calibration curve showed a strong concordance between the predicted probability of LVI/PNI and the actual probability within the dataset (p = 0.895) (Fig. 5A). The ROC curve showed the nomogram’s good discriminatory ability, with an AUC of 0.934 within the dataset (Fig. 5B). To assess how useful the nomogram was in clinical practice, DCA was conducted further. The results showed that the nomogram was particularly beneficial for predicting the risk of LVI/PNI when the predicted provability of LVI/PNI in GC patients generated by the nomogram ranged from 0.5 to 99.6% (Fig. 6).

# Discussion
Patients with GC who are positive for LVI and PNI are linked to lymph node metastasis and a poorer prognosis [17,18,19]. Consequently, preoperative identification of GC patients at high risk for LVI/PNI is crucial for tailoring personalized treatment strategies. In this study, we conducted the first evaluation of the diagnostic performance of 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT for preoperatively predicting the LVI/PNI status in GC patients. Our results demonstrate that PET parameters (SUVmax, SUVmean, and TBR) derived from preoperative 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT imaging, offer highly favorable discrimination values for predicting LVI/PNI status in GC patients, with accuracy exceeding 92% and SUVmax emerging as the most suitable predictive indicator.

LVI and PNI are commonly observed in the primary tumors of GC patients. In our study, the proportion of GC patients with LVI/PNI-positive tumors was 62.5%, which was consistent with previous research [17, 18]. LVI/PNI status was affected by some factors. Our findings confirmed that clinical characteristics such as age, sex, serum tumor markers (CEA, CA199, and CA724), and tumor location were not correlated with LVI/PNI status [20,21,22,23]. However, regarding histological grade, there was a divergence in the results. Most studies suggested that histological grade was associated with LVI/PNI status [17, 20, 22, 24], as poorly differentiated adenocarcinomas might be more likely to exhibit LVI and PNI due to their more aggressive nature. Our study found no association between histological grade and LVI/PNI status in univariate analysis, but it emerged as an independent predictor in multivariate analysis. The discrepancy in findings may be due to the interplay of various inclusion criteria.

FAP, a type II transmembrane protein and a biomarker for CAFs, is selectively over-expressed in more than 90% of epithelial tumors [25]. The positive association between FAP expression and LVI/PNI status has been well-documented [26, 27]. Radiolabeled FAP imaging has shown its potential in the assessment of gastric cancer [11,12,13,14, 28, 29]. Notably, the uptake of FAPI tracers in primary tumors has been found to strongly correlate with FAP expression levels [12, 30]. Based on these findings, we hypothesize that the high accumulation of FAPI tracer in gastric cancer is related to the LVI/PNI status. Our results supported this hypothesis, as GC patients with LVI/PNI-positive status exhibited a significantly higher uptake of 18F-FAPI-42 in their primary tumors compared to those with LVI/PNI-negative status (p < 0.001). It meant that the high SUVmax in gastric cancer had a high probability of LVI/PNI positivity. These patients with elevated SUVmax may benefit from prioritized neoadjuvant/perioperative chemotherapy (NACT), which had the potential to reduce the risk of recurrence and metastasis. Additionally, lesions positive for LVI/PNI are more likely to metastasize to lymph nodes and potentially to the peritoneum. Therefore, for patients with large lesions and high SUVmax, extensive lymph node dissection and exploration for occult peritoneal metastasis should be prepared before surgery.

A particularly noteworthy observation was that all 11 GC patients with negative 18F-FAPI-42 PET findings were also LVI/PNI-negative. This suggests that the absence of FAPI uptake in the primary tumor may indicate the absence of lymphovascular or perineural invasion in GC. Further validation with a larger sample size is warranted to confirm these preliminary findings. This finding also had significant clinical implications. FAPI-negative gastric cancer patients were all T1N0M0-stage patients. For these patients, especially those with T1a (endoscopic ultrasound diagnosis), endoscopic submucosal dissection (ESD) or surgical resection can be chosen. If pre-treatment assessment evaluated the absence of lymphovascular or perineural invasion, endoscopic treatment was a viable option. Conversely, surgical resection was recommended to minimize the risk of recurrence and metastasis. FAPI PET CT may play a role for baseline assessment.

Our research has revealed that 18F-FAPI PET/CT exhibits an exceptionally high capacity for preoperatively predicting LVI/PNI status of GC. The three PET parameters derived from 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT, SUVmax, SUVmean, and TBR, demonstrated remarkable diagnostic efficacy. These parameters achieved diagnostic accuracy exceeding 92% and sensitivity exceeding 91% in the preoperative prediction of pathological LVI/PNI status among GC patients. We attempted to combine these three parameters for enhanced prediction, but it did not significantly improve the diagnostic performance of individual parameters. As we all know, the measurement of SUVmean is easily affected by the selection of the region of interest (ROI) delineation range. Similarly, the calculation of TBR necessitates the determination of SUVmax from the gastric primary lesion and SUVmean from the normal gastric tissue. Consequently, in contrast to SUVmean and TBR, SUVmax is relatively more stable and less susceptible to subjective variations in measurement. This stability suggested that SUVmax may be the most reliable PET parameter for the preoperative prediction of LVI/PNI status in GC.

We have successfully developed a nomogram aimed at predicting the probability of LVI/PNI in GC patients. The nomogram’s performance was rigorously evaluated through the analysis of its ROC curve and calibration curve, which demonstrated its robust discriminative and calibration abilities. The nomogram was refined by incorporating two critical variables identified through stepwise regression, SUVmax and histological grade. Patients can be stratified into low-risk and high-risk categories based on their nomogram-predicted probabilities, for example, with a threshold value set at 0.65. DCA indicated that our nomogram offered clinical advantages and utility in predicting LVI/PNI status, as it provided net benefits over both a universal treatment approach and a no-treatment scenario when considering probability cutoffs ranging from 0.2 to 1.0. Collectively, our nomogram stood as a valuable tool for assessing the risk profile of patients with LVI/PNI and for guiding clinical decision-making processes. However, nomogram and DCA did not improve the diagnostic performance of individual parameters.

There are several limitations in this study. Firstly, although the results obtained in our study are ideal, the enrolled patients were from a single center and the sample size was not large enough to create a validation cohort. Therefore, a multicenter study with a larger number of patients is required to provide a more comprehensive verification of our results. Secondly, the predicative value of 18F-FAPI-42 PET parameters for LVI/PNI in GC was solely focused on gastric adenocarcinoma with the high proportion of poorly differentiated gastric adenocarcinoma and wasn’t included other histological tumor types. Further validation in a more diverse patient population with different pathological types, especially well-differentiated and moderately differentiated gastric adenocarcinoma, is needed. Thirdly, radiomics signatures based on 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT were not evaluated for predicting LVI/PNI status of GC preoperatively in this study.

Conclusion
In summary, preoperative identification of LVI/PNI status is very important for the treatment of GC. Our study first evaluated PET parameters derived from preoperative 18F-FAPI-42 PET/CT images to predict LVI/PNI status in GC patients. The results demonstrated that SUVmax, SUVmean and TBR and the nomogram all displayed favorable predictive performance. SUVmax may be the most suitable predictor. Further investigation with a larger number of patients in multiple centers should be carried out to verify our findings.
